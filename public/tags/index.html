Poetry

The Sonnets of Keris

Keris is a poet. He is well known for his role in the folktale Alisandre of the Lake, with whom he was in love with. Keris, Alisandre, and Corrinthia of the Bears were deeply embroiled in each other’s lives, living together as lovers. Below is a ballad describing a version of the tale.

Keris’ Sonnets (or Kerisian Sonnets, as they are known) are comprised of three quatrains followed by a couplet. The four lines of the quatrain alternate iambic tetrameter and iambic trimeter, respectively. The couplet is a fourteener (iambic heptameter). The rhyme scheme is ABAB CDCD EFEF GG.

Poetry

Winter Folio, Sonnet 17

The lake is like a looking glass
A silver pane of gray
She casts the vis’ge of the sky
Back into the fray.
No gentle ripple o’re her face
Serene and still she stays
I scry into her frigid depths
In search of warmer days.
And then I find in gelid plunge
My breath is borne away
But lo! a light above me shines
The promise of gold rays.
But when the surface parts for me and I draw in light wind
I find the sun was born a dream of winter gloom again.

Poetry

Summer Folio, Sonnet 13

(Commonly known as the Music Sonnet)

When first I came unto this land
Across the stormy sea
I brought my binjar and my songs
And all my harmonies.
I wandered long, a troubadour,
In search of newer sound
And in this land so large and vast
A freshness did abound.
And lo! It had me in a hold
of scales and notes so fine,
I could not help but copy-straight
So I could make it mine.
And so I pluck my strings with care and hope that you will hear
The echoes of my homeland and the love that I found here.

Summer Folio, Sonnet 1

Poetry

Winter Folio, Sonnet 1

(Commonly known as Keris’ Grief Begins)
In azure vault my lady lies,
(She thinks I do not know)
She hangs suspended like a cloud,
In a frigid sky below.
If like a stage-hand I could draw
That fractal glass aback,
I would soon soar in frozen sky
And win my lady back.
See her dance among the stars
Of winter there below?
She like a falcon dips and weaves,
Among celestial snow.
But hollow ice and hollow hearts with weight begin to crack,
And in this hollow chamber here, I know she shan’t come back.

Faith Sonnet

Faith is like a mustard seed
I’s told when I was young.
The older I have weatherd
the truer it’s become.
I miss the days when Faith and time
were blossoming and free
When I would run and play and pray
whene’er it pleaséd me.
For now my time is not my own
and neither is my faith
And I will work for forty years
before it is replaced.
I hope and pray that future times will like the mustard seed
Burst forth from hardship, toil, and pain and make just like a
tree.

and of your sins be cleansed

what plagues the soul of men
I shall not turn to wickedness
in valley dark and deep
The rod and staff my Shepherd holds
will keep me like a sheep.

Her sweat so sweet and folds so soft

My Lord is God! And holy he is mightiest on high!
Protect me will his sacred word from evil urge despised!

Mummification at Constantinople

The voice that lives inside my head
is kindest least to me
Of sorts inverted Nightingale
she leaves me there to bleed
So weak I lie in gules smeared
by self-inflicted blow
I beg her please to stitch me up
and staunch the bitter flow
Or if she won’t apply to me
triage for soldier-downed
Then may she give me final rites
and put me in the ground
Much rather I my brains and lungs all stuffed inside a jar,
Then let her venom patch me wrong and leave another scar.

No Secrets

(A Tragedy of my Own Design)

I say that I’m an open book
No secrets dark have I,
I haven’t got a poker face
And neither do I try.
I’ve learned there’s beauty in the depths
of every person’s mind
And delving in unfath’mable wit
more treasures do I find.
“Fool me once or fool me twice live
to fight another day,
Your faith in people’s long misplaced,”
I often hear them say,
“To wear your heart upon your sleeve will give you naught but grief.”
But even then when pierced my heart I find I still believe.

Heliophile

I feel each pulse inside me,
a drawing towards the light,
And steady goes my beating heart
Since end of longest night.
Each day is like a blessing
The twilight lingers just
A little more with passing days;
I revel in the dusk.
My mind has been aflame with words
And fervent they doth pour
Out of my mouth onto the page
Since winters’ losing score.
And so I write with cogent blaze and trail the sun in kind,
God, I think, it’s such a gift to even be alive.

Poetry

A Poker Game with the Anemoi

At card games I have never been
a deft or winning hand
I lack the military mind
that’s built for cards and land.
But still I fumble through the play
for neither Spring nor Fall
Nor Summer have a better mind
to win from me at all.
But Winter she’s a tactful match
and always calls my bluff
And steals my winnings like a sponge
of darker matter stuff.
But as the days go on and on, the winner’s hand goes slack
And bit by bit come longest night my daylight I win back.

Untitled Lake Sonnet / Gray Matter Sonnet

The brume and mist they hang about
My lake and frontal lobe
The sun it breaks through far too late
To brighten my abode.
Tempestuous, the clouds droop low
and lap up at the waves
Amygdala, like cloud-stirred lake
Refuses to behave.
Hippocampus, she rebels
And she unlike the lake
Has no reason to upheave
Like wind or snow or slake.
And though I have all everything that anyone could want
I find my mind is like the lake in deep gray winter months.

My Lady of the Lake --- Ode of the 520

The Ocean is a fickle Love
of salt and wind and brine
But she is next to nothing here
against this mistress mine.
My Lady she is of the lake
and mist and rain and fog
She one moment to the next
is sullen, angry, calm.
She blows no storms nor winds of strength
nor brings torrential rains
But batter she this concrete bridge
on which my love sustains.
My Lady Lake I fear her not on days when it is clear
But when she rages capped in white I know not to draw near.

Sonnet for Fall Insomnia

I find these days of autumn chill
Straight cut me to the core
My sleepless mind it drifts and drafts
From couch to chair and floor.
I think sometimes that I’m at fault
For mind-fog weary ways
And for the time and space I spend
Lost in a sleepy haze.
But still the seasons change and turn
The planet she spins on
And as the sunlight fades away
My smile goes along.
So early sleep is how I try to put myself at ease
But even then I wake to find the rest is little peace.

Sonnet for the Coming Winter

The winter in my city comes
Close on the heels of fall
A sudden leap from gold and leaf,
Into a great gray maw.
And so one day you wake to find
Your own sun you must make
And azure light becomes your ward
To keep the storm at bay.
But as the winter waxes long
The storm it gathers clouds,
The reaches of my thalamus
become a battleground.
And come the months of constant gray I toss and turn in bed,
For there is precious little sun to soothe my stormy head.

(Commonly known as Keris’ Grief Begins)
In azure vault my lady lies,
(She thinks I do not know)
She hangs suspended like a cloud,
In a frigid sky below.
If like a stage-hand I could draw
That fractal glass aback,
I would soon soar in frozen sky
And win my lady back.
See her dance among the stars
Of winter there below?
She like a falcon dips and weaves,
Among celestial snow.
But hollow ice and hollow hearts with weight begin to crack,
And in this hollow chamber here, I know she shan’t come back.

First Steps

Notes: These are collections of American Sentences that I have written over the years. This includes my own travel throughout the American Continent and my travel abroad. American sentences don’t have to be about America, I just happen to think about America a lot. American Sentences are a type of form poetry developed by Allen Ginsburg. Like a Haiku, they contain seventeen syllables. Unlike a Haiku, this form (ostensibly) better fits the abilities of the English Language. I’ve been writing these types of poems since I was fifteen or sixteen years old. I think because they are so small they must take a lot of practice to master. I think I still have a long way to go before I could even begin to consider myself a master of this form.

Faith Sonnet

Faith is like a mustard seed
I’s told when I was young.
The older I have weatherd
the truer it’s become.
I miss the days when Faith and time
were blossoming and free
When I would run and play and pray
whene’er it pleaséd me.
For now my time is not my own
and neither is my faith
And I will work for forty years
before it is replaced.
I hope and pray that future times will like the mustard seed
Burst forth from hardship, toil, and pain and make just like a
tree.

The Deep; it Beckons

The beach glows tonight. It’s a soft glow, a murmuration of light.
It’s not anything spectacular, but everything has that moonshine sheen to it. The moon hangs there in the sky, a quarter past her zenith, like some silver-glass fishing float adrift in a midnight sea.
On the shoreline an old fisherman heaves a roughened wooden rowboat towards the water. The keel of the boat gouges the sand as he drags it along, leaving behind a scar of shadow and silver. A few hours ago, the vessel had been bobbing in the surf with the pull of the moon and the will of the wind, knocking up against the knolls of beach grass some fifty yards back. Even if there had been a new moon, the summer squall that had overtaken the little cove would’ve been enough to push the tide line that high. The storm had been something fierce, cutting through the faulty shutters of the fisherman’s cottage, but the tumult had suddenly died, leaving the little inlet as still as could be. With a sudden slake give, the boat reaches the waterline, and the fisherman propels her forward, slotting right into her belly like he has done a thousand times before.
The water’s still tonight, even for a sudden clearing. It’s eerie, unnatural, and it makes the fisherman shift in his seat, acutely aware of the receding shoreline in front of him and the exposed emptiness at his back. The fisherman can count on one hand the number of times the cove’s ever been this still. He can’t see it from his seat in the boat, but the fisherman knows that to his left Jason’s Cape juts into the dark. Beyond that, the rapturous hum of the Pacific beckons him.
The little boat parts the dark saltwater-glass of moon and stars like a knife, leaving gentle ripples in her wake. They’ve been caught in a gentle current, left over from the tide’s turn. The fisherman rolls his salt-callused hands over the oars’ wood-damp handles. Now that the undercurrent has caught him, he’ll need them only to stop.


He’ll know the place.


Mary always said that the ocean called him. She would tell him that every time she found him on the porch, smoking a pipe, watching the water. “Listen to me!” she’d laugh, beckoning. “Come back to bed.” The fisherman would always laugh along with her, never saying a thing. His wife didn’t need to know she was right. The Call didn’t matter. No one else seemed to hear, and besides, there seemed more important things than heeding it. Like Mary, like his children, like making a living. But he’s old now, and his children are grown and gone and have families of their own, and since Mary’s passing the Call seems to have only gotten louder.
