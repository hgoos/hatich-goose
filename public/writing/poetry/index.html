The Sonnets of Keris

Keris is a poet. He is well known for his role in the folktale Alisandre of the Lake, with whom he was in love with. Keris, Alisandre, and Corrinthia of the Bears were deeply embroiled in each other’s lives, living together as lovers. Below is a ballad describing a version of the tale.

Keris’ Sonnets (or Kerisian Sonnets, as they are known) are comprised of three quatrains followed by a couplet. The four lines of the quatrain alternate iambic tetrameter and iambic trimeter, respectively. The couplet is a fourteener (iambic heptameter). The rhyme scheme is ABAB CDCD EFEF GG.

First Steps

Notes: These are collections of American Sentences that I have written over the years. This includes my own travel throughout the American Continent and my travel abroad. American sentences don’t have to be about America, I just happen to think about America a lot. American Sentences are a type of form poetry developed by Allen Ginsburg. Like a Haiku, they contain seventeen syllables. Unlike a Haiku, this form (ostensibly) better fits the abilities of the English Language. I’ve been writing these types of poems since I was fifteen or sixteen years old. I think because they are so small they must take a lot of practice to master. I think I still have a long way to go before I could even begin to consider myself a master of this form.

Faith Sonnet

Faith is like a mustard seed
I’s told when I was young.
The older I have weatherd
the truer it’s become.
I miss the days when Faith and time
were blossoming and free
When I would run and play and pray
whene’er it pleaséd me.
For now my time is not my own
and neither is my faith
And I will work for forty years
before it is replaced.
I hope and pray that future times will like the mustard seed
Burst forth from hardship, toil, and pain and make just like a
tree.

Nostalgia of Days

Where my father’s from it’s harvest time
The orchards rich with bounties fine

And the days are getting shorter
But I’m still getting older.

Where my mother’s from it’s raining steady
The humid heat hangs thick and heavy

And the days are getting shorter
But I’m still getting older.

Where I am from it’s turning sweet
the leaves are falling from the trees

And the days are getting shorter
But I’m still getting older.

Scarlet Letters

Unfast your heart unto the Lord
and of your sins be cleansed
For all the bile that it spews
what plagues the soul of men
I shall not turn to wickedness
in valley dark and deep
The rod and staff my Shepherd holds
will keep me like a sheep.
But Oh! Her body close to mine
the pleasures of her flesh
Her sweat so sweet and folds so soft
is where I taste her best!
My Lord is God! And holy he is mightiest on high!
Protect me will his sacred word from evil urge despised!

Mummification at Constantinople

The voice that lives inside my head
is kindest least to me
Of sorts inverted Nightingale
she leaves me there to bleed
So weak I lie in gules smeared
by self-inflicted blow
I beg her please to stitch me up
and staunch the bitter flow
Or if she won’t apply to me
triage for soldier-downed
Then may she give me final rites
and put me in the ground
Much rather I my brains and lungs all stuffed inside a jar,
Then let her venom patch me wrong and leave another scar.

No Secrets

(A Tragedy of my Own Design)

I say that I’m an open book
No secrets dark have I,
I haven’t got a poker face
And neither do I try.
I’ve learned there’s beauty in the depths
of every person’s mind
And delving in unfath’mable wit
more treasures do I find.
“Fool me once or fool me twice live
to fight another day,
Your faith in people’s long misplaced,”
I often hear them say,
“To wear your heart upon your sleeve will give you naught but grief.”
But even then when pierced my heart I find I still believe.
