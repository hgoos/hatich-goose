<span style="margin-left:2.5em;">In 1938, the dream of two Jewish boys came to fruition in the brightly colored, hot off the presses, <em>Action Comics #1.</em></span> 
    
<span style="margin-left:2.5em;">When I was 6 years old, I first read the Chronicles of Narnia.</span> It was clear to me, even as a child what the story was about. “Aslan is just Jesus,” I told my father, who seemed surprised but pleased that I’d caught on so quickly, “he’s like God.” Maybe this is where it started.

<span style="margin-left:2.5em;">In 1978, <em>Superman</em> (directed by Richard Donner and starring Christopher Reeve and Margot Kidder) was released in theaters.</span> The movie scores well on critic websites like Rotten Tomatoes, and is widely considered to be one of the best Superman adaptations ever made. 

<p style="padding-left: 40px;">“And God said, ‘Let us make man in our image, after our likeness’”
<span style="margin-left:2.5em;">-Genesis 1:26, KJV</span>
</p>

<span style="margin-left:2.5em;">When I am 19 years old, I will watch <em>Superman (1978)</em> for the first time and be utterly and entirely blown away by the beauty of the movie.</span> There is nothing particularly attractive about the film itself: the costumes are campy, the lines are cheesy, and the lighting is ethereal and dreamy. But I will be struck by the fact that I’d been missing the point of Superman for nearly all my years of reading comics. This movie will have brought a soul to Superman that I had not previously experienced. I will realize (and have endless discussions with my friend) that what makes Superman, well, super, is not the fact that he can bend steel with his bare hands or leap buildings in a single bound (although these are certainly super things), but the fact that despite all his power, despite the fact that he could end the world with one well placed punch, he still chooses to help people and have dinner with his Ma. Superman is, in fact, a super man. He is the best version of humanity, the best version of ourselves. 

<p style="padding-left: 40px;">“And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.”

<span style="margin-left:4.0em;">-John 1:14, KJV</span>
</p>
